# Orion Industrial Group — Group Treasury Policy (FICTIONAL, v2026.2)
<!-- meta: version=2026.2; effective=2026-01-01; owner=Group Treasury; classification=FICTIONAL (evaluation) -->

## TP-1.1 Purpose
This policy governs cash, liquidity, funding, foreign exchange and payment authorisations for all
entities of Orion Industrial Group. Group Treasury (US HoldCo) operates as the in-house bank.

## TP-2.1 Approval matrix for treasury actions
All movements of money require human approval; no system may execute payments autonomously.
Intercompany transfers up to USD 10 million require approval by the Group Treasurer.
Intercompany transfers above USD 10 million require approval by the Chief Financial Officer.
Any voluntary prepayment or drawdown under the revolving credit facility requires CFO approval.
Any cash movement out of an entity subject to capital controls requires CFO approval and Tax sign-off.

## TP-2.2 Segregation of duties
The person or system that proposes a treasury action may not approve it. Approvals are recorded in
the treasury audit log with the approver's identity, timestamp and rationale.

## TP-2.3 Early payment of suppliers
Early payment to capture a supplier discount is permitted where the implied annualised return
exceeds the Group's marginal cost of funds (the revolver rate) and the paying entity remains above its
operating buffer. Individual early payments up to USD 2 million may be approved by the Group Treasurer.

## TP-3.1 Operating cash buffers
Each operating subsidiary must maintain a minimum operating cash buffer equal to the greater of
USD 1.5 million or 0.6 weeks of revenue, measured on a conservative (P10) forecast basis.
Group Treasury must maintain a minimum buffer of USD 25 million.

## TP-3.2 Intercompany funding
Intercompany funding may only be provided to cover operating needs, including restoring the
operating buffer. Borrowing to place cash in higher-yielding local deposits is prohibited.

## TP-4.1 Cash concentration
Cash above operating buffers should be concentrated at Group Treasury where legally and economically
efficient, taking into account foreign-exchange spreads, withholding taxes and transfer costs.
Concentrated surplus should first be applied to reduce revolver borrowings.

## TP-3.3 Management minimum liquidity
Group liquidity (unrestricted cash plus undrawn committed facilities) must be managed to remain above an
internal floor of USD 300 million on the P10 forecast path — USD 50 million above the RCF-22.1 covenant.
A forecast breach of this floor requires a funding plan approved by the CFO.

## TP-5.1 Investment of surplus cash
Surplus cash not required to reduce borrowings may be invested only in AAA-rated money-market funds,
with no more than 30% of invested cash in a single fund and a maximum tenor of three months.

## TP-5.2 Bank counterparty limits
No single bank may hold more than 40% of Group cash. Banks rated below A- may hold no more than 15%.
Breaches must be remedied within 30 days through a rebalancing plan approved by the Group Treasurer.

## TP-6.1 Collections
Invoices predicted to be paid more than 45 days late, or disputed invoices above USD 1 million, must
be escalated to the regional finance director within five business days.

## TP-7.1 Use of artificial intelligence
AI systems may analyse data, forecast and recommend actions. They may not approve or execute payments.
Personal data and bank account identifiers must be masked before being sent to external AI services.
All AI recommendations must cite the data and policy clauses on which they rely.

## TP-7.2 Payment release and dual control
Approval of a payment does not release it. Payment files are prepared as drafts, validated and released
by a second authorised signatory (Group Treasurer or CFO) who is neither the requester nor the approver,
through the bank channel specified in the bank mandate. No system may transmit payments autonomously.
